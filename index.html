Chapter III

Quartz:
a crystal that counts

A sliver of quartz, cut into a tuning fork, flexes 32,768 times a second when voltage is applied. That number is 215 — so fifteen flip-flops halving the signal yield exactly one pulse per second.

Complexity
—

Binary divider · 15 stages

Each stage toggles at half the rate of the one before it. Stage 15 flips once per second and fires the Lavet stepping motor, which advances the seconds hand by one step.

±15 sper month — a typical quartz tolerance (≈ ±0.5 s/day)
±5 sper year — thermocompensated HAQ quartz (e.g. Grand Seiko 9F, Citizen 0100 claims ±1 s/yr)
1969Seiko Astron 35SQ — the first quartz wristwatch, sold on Christmas Day in Tokyo

Chapter V

The Swiss lever
escapement

The single most important mechanism in a mechanical watch: it lets the mainspring's energy escape one tooth at a time, while giving the balance a tiny push to keep it swinging. Slow it down and watch the five phases.

Complexity
0.12×
01 · LockingAn escape tooth rests on the pallet stone's locking face. The train is frozen; the balance swings freely — the "detached" part of the detached lever.
02 · UnlockingThe balance's impulse jewel (ruby pin) enters the fork slot and nudges the lever, sliding the stone off the tooth.
03 · ImpulseThe released tooth slides across the stone's angled impulse face, pushing the fork — which pushes the ruby pin — giving the balance its energy.
04 · DropThe tooth leaves the stone and the wheel jumps freely until the next tooth lands on the opposite pallet. This is the "tick".
05 · DrawUndercut locking faces pull the fork against its banking pin, holding it safely out of the balance's way until the return swing.

Chapter VII

Complications,
working

Anything beyond hours, minutes and seconds is a complication. Every module below is live — computed from your clock, the Moon's actual synodic cycle, and the Gregorian calendar. Press the pushers. Strike the repeater.

Complexity
Chronograph+ ~100 parts

A stopwatch built into the movement. A column wheel or cam coordinates start, stop and reset; a clutch (horizontal or vertical) couples the chrono seconds to the running train. Shown: 1/8 s resolution, 30-minute counter.

Moon phaseLive · —

A 59-tooth disc advanced once per day carries two moons; each lunation is approximated as 29.5 days, drifting 1 day every 2 years 7 months. High-precision 135-tooth trains drift only 1 day in 122 years. Real synodic month: 29.530589 days.

GMT / Dual timeUTC

A fourth hand rotates once in 24 hours against a 24-hour bezel. The arrow-tipped hand here points to UTC; the bezel shows your offset. "True" GMT lets the local hour hand jump independently in 1-hour steps.

Perpetual calendar—

A mechanical memory of the Gregorian calendar: a 48-month cam "knows" month lengths and leap years and needs no correction until 2100 — the secular year that skips its leap day.

Tourbillon1 rev / 60 s

Abraham-Louis Breguet's 1801 patent: the entire escapement rides in a rotating cage, averaging out positional errors caused by gravity when a pocket watch sat vertical. Today: the ultimate display of finishing skill.

Power reserve—

A differential gear compares barrel arbor (winding) and barrel drum (unwinding) rotation, driving a hand that shows how much energy remains in the mainspring.

Minute repeaterGrand complication

On demand, a slide on the case arms the strike train; snail-shaped cams read the hands' positions, and two hammers strike gongs coiled around the movement: low tones for hours, ding-dong pairs for quarters, high tones for minutes.

Chapter IX

Beyond the gear:
light, liquid crystal, silicon

The quartz crisis rewrote the rules. Red LEDs that lit only on demand, liquid crystal that never sleeps, then computers on the wrist. Push the buttons — they work.

Complexity
1970 · LEDPress to read

Light-emitting diodes drew so much current that the display stayed dark until you pressed the button. Hamilton's Pulsar (1970) cost more than a Rolex at launch.

1973 · LCDTIME

WATER RESISTMODE

Twisted-nematic liquid crystals between crossed polarisers rotate light 90° until a voltage untwists them — each black segment is just light that can no longer pass. Microwatts of power; years of battery. Buttons: L light, M mode, S start/stop.

2015 → · SmartwatchAMOLED

An application processor, sensors (optical heart rate, accelerometer, gyroscope, GNSS) and an OLED where each pixel is its own light. Accuracy is borrowed: the phone syncs it to network time, itself traceable to atomic clocks.
